# 01 — Brand Foundation

## Name

**Rizzotto**

[RIZZOTTO-ETYMOLOGY — Luke ergänzt]

The visual brand is **Rizzotto** (domain: rizzotto.gg). The Khazalid
atmospheric layer — *Karaz Ankor*, *Roll of Honour*, *Conclave*, Latin
mottos — is preserved in the UI as ceremonial decoration, not as brand
identity. See [02-voice.md](./02-voice.md) for the full lexicon.

## Mission

> **Forge a place where every Old-World marshal can prove their list — in
> living combat, recorded forever in stone.**

We are a competitive home for Warhammer: The Old World tournaments. We treat
the results with the gravity they deserve: rankings, drafts, and brackets are
*carved*, not casually rolled.

## Vision

A platform where the act of submitting an army, drafting a list, and winning a
muster feels *consequential*. Where opening the website feels like stepping
into a stone cloister at first light — quiet, weighty, and immediately
recognizable.

## Personality

Five adjectives. If a design choice does not embody at least three, reject it.

- **Forged** — Made by hand. Hammered. Bears toolmarks.
- **Reverent** — Carries history. Speaks softly. Honors the dead.
- **Deliberate** — Slow on purpose. No wasted motion. No clutter.
- **Heraldic** — Symbolic, layered, decipherable to those who study it.
- **Beautiful in its brutality** — Not grimdark for the sake of it. Grimdark
  with golden hour rim-light.

## Brand Pillars

Four operating principles that shape every product decision.

### 1. Stone before flash

We choose weight and stability over animation tricks. A button that *feels
heavy* on click is worth ten that whoosh. Motion exists, but it is rare and
intentional — like a chisel-strike.

### 2. The list is sacred

Every army list a player submits is a small artifact. We display them with
typography, breathing room, and structure that respects the labor. We never
treat a roster as a JSON dump.

### 3. History writes itself

Triumphs, falls, drafts — once recorded, they are immutable visual artifacts.
The Roll of Honour is *literally* presented as if carved. Past tournaments
remain browsable, never archived-away, never demoted.

### 4. Latin / Khazalid is decoration, not gatekeeping

Mottos exist for atmosphere. They never carry critical UI meaning. A non-lore
visitor can navigate the entire site without translating a single
non-English word.

## Anti-Brand — what we are not

The fastest way to stay on-brand is to know what to refuse.

| We are **not** ❌                          | Because                                                          |
|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Bright fantasy / "WoW gaming" colors       | We are anthracite + one warm spark, not rainbow gradient.        |
| Cartoon / chibi / emoji-heavy              | We do not use emoji in product UI. Lucide icons only.            |
| Loud marketing-speak ("Crush your foes!")  | Voice is restrained, archaic, never imperative-shouty.           |
| Memeable / ironic                          | Rizzotto takes itself seriously, on purpose.                     |
| Single-faction-coded (Empire-bias etc.)    | Visual identity reads neutral; no faction wins the palette.      |
| Glassmorphism-spa wellness aesthetic       | No frosted-glass cards. We use **stone**, not glass.             |
| Cute mascots / kawaii                      | The only animate creature in our brand is a knight without a face. |
| Trend-chasing (vaporwave, Y2K, brutalist) | These will date. Heraldry has not dated in 800 years.            |

## Audience

- **Primary**: Warhammer: The Old World tournament-active players. Adults, lore-aware,
  often miniature painters. They will recognize a Khazalid pun. They will be
  disappointed by anything generic.
- **Secondary**: Casual TOW players curious about competitive play. They should
  feel welcomed by usability — not gatekept by the lore.
- **Tertiary**: Tournament organizers running events. They need *trust* — the
  platform must look like it will still exist next year.

## Voice in one paragraph

We speak the way a marshal addresses their order before muster: precise,
economical, never warm-and-fuzzy. We use archaic words for ceremonial moments
(*Take Up Arms*, *Roll of Honour*, *The Conclave*) and plain modern English
for everything functional (*Search*, *Filter*, *Settings*). We *never* use
exclamation marks in product copy. Headlines lean Latin or Khazalid for
flavor; body copy is clear English. See [02-voice.md](./02-voice.md).
